BRIDGE + 4 STEPS

From painful first steps to a clear plan, often the same day

We don't guess. We assess, explain what we find, and walk you through your options before you leave.

1. Full heel assessment

We look at how you move, where the pain sits, and what's loading the tissue. We ask about your work, your shoes, and when it hurts most.

2. Diagnosis, and imaging if we need it

Most heel pain has a name. Plantar fasciitis, heel spur, arch overload, knowing which one matters, because the right management is different for each. If we need imaging to confirm what's happening, we'll tell you exactly what and why.

3. Your treatment options, explained honestly

We walk you through what's available, from a simple loading plan through to shockwave therapy or custom orthotics where those are the right fit. Not everyone needs orthotics. We'll tell you honestly whether you do. No upselling. No one-size plan.

4. A plan you can actually follow

You leave knowing what to do, what to expect, and when to come back. If something's not working, we adjust.

WHO IS THIS FOR

  • You get heel pain first thing in the morning, that sharp bite with the first few steps
  • It eases once you're moving, but it's back by the end of the day
  • You've had it for weeks, maybe months, it settles, then something brings it back
  • It gets worse after a long shift, a walk, or a day on your feet
  • You feel it in your heel, your arch, or along the bottom of your foot
  • You want to know what is actually causing it, not just another thing to try

If three or more of those sound like you, book an assessment. One visit tells you where you stand.

WHY IT KEEPS COMING BACK

You rest it. It feels better.

You go back to your day, and it's there again by evening.

That's not bad luck. It's what happens when the load on your foot never changes.

Rest takes the edge off. It doesn't touch the reason it started.

You've tried stretching. New shoes. The gel insoles from the pharmacy. Maybe weeks off, and it still came back.

The months stack up. You shift your weight to protect one side. Your knee starts to ache. Your back tightens. You stop doing the things you used to do without thinking.

That's not healing. That's your body compensating.

The difference between heel pain that drags on for a year and heel pain that settles in weeks is usually a clear diagnosis and a plan built around your life, not generic advice.
